**Status**: seeded by the lead 2026-11-10; phases expanded by the planner 2026-11-10 (iteration 1 below); revision 1 corrections applied by the planner 2026-11-10 (iteration 2 below — corrections only, nothing built); revision 2 applied by the planner 2026-11-10 (iteration 3 below — the B2 coverage fix and two rulings, nothing built); not built.
**Base commit**: `eee2179a` (branch `claude_develop`).
**Parents**: handover `.work/watch-handover-2026-11-10.md` (recommendation D of `.work/watch-app-overview-2026-11-10.md`); plans 128, 141 (owed end), 98, 136 (timers).

## Overview

The owner cannot release the watch app because the phone and the wrist both edit one live workout over an unreliable radio and nothing proves they end up agreeing. Every report so far was a new corner of the same drift. This plan builds the thing that finds the next corner before a device does: a seeded, repeatable test that runs random actions on both devices, with random radio faults and app kills, then checks that both sides agree.

It is **test-only**: no file under `lib/` or `watch/` changes. What it finds is the input to plan 146 ("a finished workout is final", PROTOCOL.md + both engines). The Swift engine is not exercised here; the Dart twin of the wrist (`lib/watch/session/`) is the reference model, and a minimal failing trace found here is later replayed on Swift as a fixture (not in this plan).

Owner decision 2026-11-10: a finished or discarded workout is never reopened. The test therefore treats `completed` and `abandoned` as final.

What the owner sees: nothing in the app. They see, in the evidence file, how many seeds agree, and for each disagreement a short numbered trace ("1. phone starts. 2. link drops. 3. wrist finishes. 4. old 'started' message arrives. Result: wrist active again") that a person can read.

## Input-reality checklist

- **Writers.** Two writers per session; the radio is the only shared channel; no shared memory between the two halves in the harness (a frame is a deep-copied JSON map, as on the wire).
- **Lifecycle.** The wrist kill keeps the store and drops all in-memory state; a phone kill is NOT modelled in this plan (the shipping graph's restart path needs its own harness); say so in the evidence.
- **Time edges.** All times from the injected clock; a frame's `sentAt` is the sender's clock at send; no clock skew modelled (a skew case is a follow-up).
- **Load.** 200 seeds × up to 40 actions, with the heal; budget 60 s.

| D-14512 | The harness radio reproduces the OS queue's **withdraw rules** verbatim — D-14504's first clause says it models only what the protocol says the OS does, and withdrawal is part of that. Phone→wrist queue: queueing a `session_snapshot` or `session_lifecycle` withdraws the queued copy of the same type for the same `sessionId`; queueing a `structure_change` withdraws the queued copy with the same `sessionId` and payload `changeId`; `receipt` and `exercise_push` are never withdrawn. Without the withdraw rules the run manufactures late frames the OS itself destroys, and every finding is suspect | PROTOCOL.md:863 (2026-10-23 row) and :864 (2026-10-24 row); the real transport's withdraw: `lib/core/platform/watch_transport.dart:251-257`, `:369-398` |
