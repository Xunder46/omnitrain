## Goal and acceptance criteria
Owner QA (2026-10-08): "if the phone or watch are locked, they never sync again even with the sync button"; "finishing on the watch keeps the phone session screen open". Root cause read from code: a frame sent while the other device is not reachable is dropped without anyone being told, and the phone's bookkeeping then believes it was delivered.
- AC-1 A frame sent to an unreachable counterpart is reported as NOT delivered, to its caller, on both sides (phone Dart, wrist Swift/Dart).
- AC-2 The phone's push keeps owing what was not delivered (its snapshot, a session end, the 19a reset, a deletion) and sends it at the next trigger once the watch can be reached; nothing is marked sent that was not.
- AC-3 A workout ended on the watch while the phone could not be reached is announced to the phone at the watch's next catch-up, and the phone's session ends and its screen leaves (19a D-174).
- AC-4 Nothing changes for a reachable pair: same frames, same order, no duplicates.
Out of scope: keeping the watch app alive while the wrist is down (19c: HealthKit workout session or extended runtime session = app capability + provisioning change = owner's step), the watch menu (20), timed Start->Log (21).

## Decision Ledger
- D-190 Delivery has a result. The phone's `WatchConnectivityTransport.send` (`lib/core/platform/watch_transport.dart:141-149`) catches every error and returns normally, never reads reachability, and the plugin's iOS `sendMessage` (`watch_connectivity-0.2.8/ios/.../WatchConnectivityPlugin.swift:46-47`) passes neither a reply nor an error handler, so a frame to an unreachable watch vanishes while the Dart future completes. New rule: the transport checks `_channel.isReachable` first; when the counterpart is not reachable (or the channel throws) it does NOT call the channel and the caller learns it (shape is the planner's call: a typed outcome or a thrown `WatchUndelivered`; every existing caller of `WatchTransport.send`/`sendState`/`reportLifecycleFor`/`applyStructureChange` is listed in the Impact table). Reachability is read per send, never cached (`_phoneReachable` is a hint for the UI only). The wrist side already throws `RadioError.phoneNotReachable` (`ios/OmniTrain Watch App/OmniTrainWatchConnectivity.swift:~88-95`) and reports-and-drops (`reportWatchRadioFailure`): the bridge/forwarder (`WatchEmitForwarder`) must surface the same "undelivered" to the engine's owed-set logic where it keeps one.
- D-191 The phone's push advances no bookkeeping on an undelivered frame. `WatchSessionAutoPush._pushOnce` (`lib/state/watch/watch_session_auto_push.dart:236-239`) sets `_baseline = encoded` BEFORE `_mirror.sendState(composed)`: with D-190 it sets the baseline only after a delivered outcome, so an undelivered snapshot is composed and offered again at the next trigger. The same for: pending session ends (`_announceEnd` / `_pendingEnds`: removed only when the end frame was delivered), the 19a reset debt (`owesResetFor`; D-176 keeps it owed), owed deletions (`_owed`, F7 already keeps them on a thrown send; an undelivered outcome is the same case), and `WatchSyncGraph.sync()`'s own-projection send. 17a/17b rules stay (debounce, payload-equality gate, `sendTimeout`, ever-seen set, rebaseline after an applied wrist frame).
- D-192 Triggers that re-offer what is owed (no timer, no scheduler of its own): (a) any WorkoutState-driven push pass (existing), (b) phone app resume (`WatchResumeSync` -> `WatchSyncGraph.sync()`, existing), (c) ANY frame arriving from the wrist — it proves the radio works in that direction right now: after the router has applied it, if anything is owed the push runs a pass (bounded, coalesced by the existing debounce; the arriving frame itself is never echoed: D-82 `rebaseline` rule stays for what the frame just brought in), (d) the answer to a wrist announcement/request (19a D-182, existing). A pass with nothing owed sends nothing (S-207).
- D-193 The wrist's owed set covers the end. Observations are already owed until confirmed (`WatchSyncOrchestrator.catchUp` -> `sync` replays them, `WatchSyncOrchestrator.swift:~116-128`; engine `pruneConfirmed` only drops confirmed rows). A session End pressed on the wrist while the phone is unreachable (`WatchEffortRating.end()` -> engine `transitionTo(status: completed)` -> `emitLifecycle` + snapshot, both dropped) must reach the phone at the wrist's next catch-up: the planner verifies what the wrist re-announces at catch-up for a session whose status is no longer `active` (`engine.sessionSnapshot()`, `answerSnapshotRequest`, `WatchSessionEngine.swift` lifecycle emit) and adds the missing announcement (a snapshot carrying status `completed`/`abandoned`, or a lifecycle replay) on BOTH stacks, additive, with the phone ending its copy through `WatchSessionAdoptionBridge.onLifecycle` / snapshot status handling exactly as for a live lifecycle frame (19a D-174 then leaves the screen). No new frame family if an existing one carries it.
- D-194 No protocol change unless a scenario forces one (v1 unreleased: any change is additive and dated in PROTOCOL.md with both validators and fixtures updated in the same phase). `updateApplicationContext`/`transferUserInfo` are NOT used in 19b (D-97 reasons stand: the shell has no `didReceiveApplicationContext`, the plugin has no `transferUserInfo`); the re-offer triggers of D-192 are the convergence mechanism.
- D-195 Honest results are also visible to tests: the existing fakes (`FakeWatchConnectivitySession` Swift, the Dart `WatchMessageChannel` fakes in `test/`) gain a reachability switch so every scenario below runs without a device.

- D-196 D-190's shape is a typed outcome the caller reads, not a thrown error: new file `lib/core/platform/watch_delivery.dart` with `enum WatchDelivery { delivered, undelivered }`. `delivered` means "handed to the platform radio while the counterpart was reachable" — the strongest claim the platform supports (neither the plugin's Dart surface nor WCSession carries an acknowledgement; `lib/core/platform/watch_connectivity_channel.dart` calls `sendMessage` with no reply handler) — and it never means "the counterpart applied it". `undelivered` means "not handed over": the counterpart was not reachable, or the radio refused the frame. `WatchMirrorTransport.send` (`lib/state/watch/live_session_mirror_state.dart:36`) becomes `Future<WatchDelivery> send(...)`; no send throws for a non-delivery, so a caller that ignores the result (`lib/state/watch/watch_sync_request_handler.dart:104`) behaves exactly as it does today. The wrist's `WatchSyncTransport.send` (`lib/watch/start/watch_sync_orchestrator.dart:24`, `WatchSyncOrchestrator.swift:37`) stays `Future<void>`: the wrist's owed set is built from stored, unconfirmed rows (`engine.pendingObservations()`, `pruneConfirmed`), its radio throws `RadioError.phoneNotReachable` before `sendMessage` (`ios/OmniTrain Watch App/OmniTrainWatchConnectivity.swift:~88-95`) and the forwarder reports the refusal, so a typed result has nothing to feed there. Dart's `void` being a top type is what lets one class implement both interfaces: `WatchConnectivityTransport.send` returns `Future<WatchDelivery>` and still satisfies `WatchSyncTransport.send`.
- D-197 D-191's sites, one for one — and what is deliberately not a site. The mirror's owed senders return the result: `sendState` (`live_session_mirror_state.dart:327`), `deleteEntryAs` (`:482`) and `reportLifecycleFor` (`:547`) become `Future<WatchDelivery>`; `reportLifecycle` (`:521`), `applyStructureChange` (`:362`) and `pushExercise` keep their signatures and discard the result (no owed set reads them; the only value-reader in the repository is `test/live_mirroring_test.dart:764`). (1) `_baseline = encoded` (`watch_session_auto_push.dart:227-245`) only after `delivered`. (2) `_pendingEnds.remove(sessionId)` in `_announceEnd` (`:393-440`) only after `delivered`, in both terminal branches — the gone-row `reportLifecycleFor(abandoned)` branch and the `reportLifecycleFor(completed)` branch — while `completeSession()`'s own frame is not an owed site: after the phone's own end the next pass composes the ended session and offers it as a snapshot, which is site (1); S-217 pins that frame and its baseline. (3) `owed.remove(entryId)` in `_announceDeletions` (`:280-333`) only after `delivered` — an `undelivered` return takes the path a thrown send takes today (F7's rule: still owed, re-announced with the same changeId), and the `finally` that restores `_announced` stays. (4) `WatchSyncGraph.sync()`'s own-projection send (`watch_sync_wiring.dart:105-108`): on `undelivered` it calls the new `WatchSessionAutoPush.forgetBaseline()` so the next trigger composes and offers the projection again. (5) 19a's reset step (D-176): its `abandoned(W)` frame and P's snapshot are re-offered whole and in order, and the debt clears only once P's snapshot is `delivered` (D-202). 17a/17b rules stay exactly as they are: debounce, payload-equality gate, `_sendTimeout`, the ever-seen set, `rebaseline` after an applied wrist frame (D-82).
- D-198 Where 19b's two new phone-side call sites live. `WatchSessionAutoPush.forgetBaseline()` (public, beside `flush()`, `watch_session_auto_push.dart:338`) clears `_baseline` only: it sends nothing, schedules nothing, and is called from exactly one place — `WatchSyncGraph.sync()`'s `undelivered` branch. The re-offer after a wrist frame is `await push.rebaseline(); await push.flush();` at the end of `createWatchSync`'s `onIncoming` closure (`watch_sync_wiring.dart:212-221`), in that order: the frame that just arrived proves the radio works in that direction, so what is owed goes out, but what that frame brought in is the new baseline and is never echoed (D-82). `flush()`'s existing `_draining`/`_again` guard is the coalescer D-192 asks for.
- D-199 D-193's missing announcement is a lifecycle replay from the wrist's own stored terminal row, on both stacks. New method `replaySessionEnd()` on `WatchSessionEngine` (Swift `watch/watchos/Sources/WatchSessionEngine/WatchSessionEngine.swift`; twin `lib/watch/session/watch_session_engine.dart`): when the held session's status is terminal (`completed` or `abandoned`) it composes the frame with the engine's existing row-driven composer (`lifecycle(_:state:)` `WatchSessionEngine.swift:1742-1767`; twin `_lifecycle` `:1649`) over the stored terminal row and emits it through the engine's existing validated emit path (`emitLifecycle` `:1141-1148`; the twin emits at `:1190-1195`), so the frame carries the live frame's own identity — `messageId = "msg-<recordId>"`, `sentAt` and `payload.at` = the row's `recordedAt` — and mints nothing. For an `active` status, or no session, it emits nothing. `WatchSyncOrchestrator.sync` (Swift `:88-103`; twin `:74-90`) calls it after the owed observations and before the snapshot branch, gated on the reachability already read into `paths.phoneReachable`: while the pair is apart nothing is composed, nothing is queued and nothing is reported, and the next catch-up asks again. No new frame family, no new protocol field (D-194), no in-memory queue — the wrist's owed set stays storage-backed (D-196). The phone needs no new rule: the router already routes `session_lifecycle` to `WatchSessionAdoptionBridge.onLifecycle` (`watch_incoming_router.dart:120-123`), whose guards make a duplicate a provable no-op (`held == null || held.id != sessionId` → return; `held.endedAtMs != null` → return), and `consider` (`:562-641`) returns `notRunning` for a non-active status, so a snapshot can never end the phone's copy.
- D-200 D-195's doubles, concretely: every Dart transport double answers from its own switch — `delivered` unless a case set it otherwise — and every double records what it was asked to carry, so a test can assert "the channel was not called" rather than only "nothing was applied"; every double's reachability defaults to reachable, so no existing case changes. The doubles: `test/watch_transport_test.dart`'s `_Endpoint`/`_Link` (already reachability-aware, `:124-180`), `test/watch_session_auto_push_test.dart`'s `_PhoneRadio` (`:210`, its `send` `:252`), `test/watch_session_projection_test.dart`'s `_PhoneRadio` (`:425`), `test/watch_session_adoption_build_notify_test.dart`'s `_PhoneRadio` (`:94`), `test/helpers/live_session_fixtures.dart`'s `RecordingMirrorTransport` (`:10`), `test/helpers/watch_capture_import_harness.dart`'s `CaptureTransport` (`:99`), `test/live_mirroring_test.dart`'s `_PhoneTransport`/`_RecordingTransport` (`:278`, `:300`) and `test/phone_manage_bridge_test.dart`'s `_PhoneTransport` (`:197`). The Swift double `FakeWatchConnectivitySession` already carries `isPhoneReachable`/`reportReachable`/`sendError`; a Swift case that needs one adds it there.
- D-201 Reporting rule (derived from D-190's "reported as NOT delivered"; flagged vetoable — Open question 3). A frame the radio refuses is `undelivered` **and reported** once through the existing failure hook (`WatchConnectivityTransport._report` on the phone, the forwarder's `onFailure` on the wrist). An unreachable counterpart is `undelivered` and **quiet**: no report, no log, no counter, no retry inside the pass — otherwise a locked wrist produces one report per pass, which is the noise the brief's trap (e) forbids. The owed set carries the frame; the next trigger sends it.
- D-202 19b composes with 19a's reset (D-176) as one delivery step: the `abandoned(W)` frame, P's snapshot and the trailing `requestSnapshot()` keep their order and their change ids, and the step is re-offered whole — never half — at the next D-192 trigger while any frame in it is `undelivered`; the debt clears only when P's snapshot is `delivered`. 19b therefore lands after 19a Phase 5 (its `catchUp(reachable:)` and the D-182 answer are two of 19b's triggers), and in a reset the single `requestSnapshot()` (19a Phase 2 item 4) stays single.
- D-203 The answers deliberately not owed, so the reviewer reads them as decisions rather than omissions: `WatchSyncRequestHandler._sendSnapshot` (`watch_sync_request_handler.dart:104`) discards the result — the request arrived over the same radio, so an answer the radio refuses is re-asked by the wrist's own next catch-up (19a D-183); `reportLifecycle`, `applyStructureChange` and `pushExercise` keep their signatures (D-197); `watch_incoming_router.dart` and `watch_session_adoption_bridge.dart` are unchanged by 19b; and nothing in 19b touches `lib/data/models/models.dart` or `scripts/sqlite_schema.sql` (no persisted field changes), so the SQL contract test is outside this PR's blast radius.

## Requirements
- R-1 (D-190, D-196) Every phone-side send returns a `WatchDelivery`: reachable and the channel accepted it → `delivered`; unreachable → `undelivered` with the channel never called; the channel threw → `undelivered` and reported once. No send throws for a non-delivery.
- R-2 (D-191, D-197) No piece of phone bookkeeping advances on an `undelivered` frame: the push baseline, the pending session ends, the owed deletions, 19a's reset debt, and `WatchSyncGraph.sync()`'s own projection.
- R-3 (D-192, D-198) What is owed is re-offered at the existing triggers only — a WorkoutState pass, a resume `sync()`, any frame from the wrist (after it is applied and rebaselined), and the answer to a wrist announcement. No timer, no scheduler, no queue; a pass with nothing owed sends nothing.
- R-4 (D-193, D-199) A session that ended on the wrist reaches the phone at the wrist's next catch-up, on both stacks, as the session's own lifecycle frame, composed from the stored terminal row and emitted through the engine's existing path; the phone's copy ends and its screen leaves through the existing bridge path.
